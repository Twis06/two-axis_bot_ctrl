# Packet 2B: fault classes, recovery and information access

Scope and acceptance criteria: [EXECUTION_PLAN.md](../../docs/process/EXECUTION_PLAN.md) §5, Packet 2B. Evidence labels follow the [evidence conventions](../README.md#evidence-conventions). Every number here is **Simulated** or **Calculated**; none is a hardware observation. All numbers come from the pre-freeze working controller and are diagnostic, not final evidence. Frozen evidence is regenerated in Packet 2C.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Fault recovery cannot bypass the condition that caused the fault.
- Communication loss cannot be concealed by fresh outgoing commands.
- The claimed operating mode uses no unavailable future information.
- Fallback is characterized as motion reduction, not as a safe pose.

## 3. Design decisions

### Recovery by fault class (Proposed design, implemented)

**Drive.** Every re-arm needs:

- fresh, valid commands whose reference is aligned with the axis (|q − q_ref| < 2°) for 50 ms;
- the tripping condition to have cleared.

Re-arm conditions by class:

| Class | Faults | Additional re-arm condition |
|---|---|---|
| comm | `cmd_timeout`, `invalid_command` | none |
| motion | `overspeed` | speed back below 0.8·v_max |
| thermal | `overtemp` | winding below trip − 10 K |
| tracking | `watchdog_trip`, and any unknown name | the command carries `ack == fault_id` |

Three tracking or motion latches within 30 s lock the drive out for the run.

**Host, after a tracking fault:**

1. It suspends the request. The governor brakes at constant deceleration and then holds; the path clock is frozen.
2. It acknowledges the fault only while `catch_plan` predicts a brake-to-rest from the measured state that fits. The measured state is (q, v), with v from a Kalman estimate of roll. "Fits" means:
   - the braking fits within actuator capacity, including a coupling bound (the decaying recent peak of the predicted coupling, not its instantaneous value);
   - the stop ends inside the holdable interval;
   - the final hold plus that coupling bound fits within capacity.

   The check is re-evaluated every tick. The acknowledgement is withdrawn (ack = 0) as soon as it fails, and the drive needs the ack on every command of its 50 ms re-arm dwell.

   *Changed in review round 1.* The first version acknowledged once and never withdrew it (I1). It also judged the hold without coupling and braking against the budget, and its verdict flipped with the sign of a 0.01 rad/s velocity (I2). Capacity is used because the alternative to a catch is passive damping, which resists neither load nor coupling; the lockout bounds repeated failures.
3. The reference then starts at the measured position and velocity.
4. The original request resumes only after `replan()`. A lockout is reported as a rejection, and `replan()` does not clear it.

After a comm fault, the host re-aligns and the drive re-arms on its own, as before.

**Iteration: a first draft also required the axis at rest (|v| < 0.5 rad/s) before re-arming.** It was removed on evidence:

- E +35 mm: the axis fell to its passive rest, travelling 51° median and 96° maximum.
- C +35 mm without a yaw planner: continuing coupling kept the damped axis moving. It stayed in passive fallback for 97.6% of the remaining run and reached 79.5° (seed 1).

The catch replaces that rule. The first case now travels at most 9.9° and the second is back under control after the comm re-arm. Both are regression tests.

**The catch is a model prediction, not a guarantee.** Repeated failures are bounded by the lockout.

### Information mode (design decision under the plan's scope guard)

- **Default (`yaw_info="estimate"`):** the coupling feed-forward is predicted at t + T_act from the yaw encoder in Feedback. It uses a three-state constant-acceleration Kalman filter at the drive timestamps, with q_jerk = 1e5, followed by constant-acceleration extrapolation. It never reads the yaw plan, and a test fails if it does.
- **Parameter choice:**
  - q_jerk was chosen on the B/C sines from 1e4–1e8. It leaves 10–13% of the coupling RMS (Simulated prototype).
  - The 12 ms warm-up is the measured convergence time from a cold start. The filter restarts after a gap longer than 50 ms.
  - It is deterministic state estimation, not the Task 3 learning feature.
- **Optional (`yaw_info="plan"`):** the pre-2B look-ahead of the host's own yaw plan. It assumes the yaw axis follows its plan. §5.4 tests it with a yaw that lags 10 ms and moves 10% more than planned. A non-finite plan sample falls back to the estimate for that tick and is reported.
- **Architecture:** the controller structure is unchanged; only the source of qy_d and qy_dd changes.

### No yaw authority

Incompatible coupling without a yaw planner is handled as follows:

- The host keeps active control, suspends the roll request (it brakes and holds), and records a coordinated-stop request (`coord_stop`). It no longer hands over to passive fallback.
- It declares incompatibility immediately only when the coupling exceeds actuator capacity for a *static hold* at the reference, on both the normal and drive-fallback paths. Otherwise a 0.3 s escalation timer runs.
- False triggers found and removed:
  - the reference's own transient join demand counted against capacity;
  - the fallback path used the budget, not capacity;
  - the estimate's 12 ms warm-up was not respected;
  - after review (I3): during the governor's own transients (a join after a realign, a stop, a start blend), over-budget was blamed on yaw. Coupling is now judged against the static hold room at the reference, the escalation timer does not run, and the yaw monitor's history is cleared.
- The roll axis alone cannot contain continuing external yaw disturbance beyond its capacity. That case is reported, not claimed.

### Yaw monitor

- Every valid host sample counts in the denominator. Only samples saturated while yaw coupling is significant count in the numerator.
- The history is cleared whenever the host idles. A window spanning an outage mixes two regimes; the shrink hold-off survives the clear.
- A decision needs at least 90% of the window's expected samples.
- After review (I3): the history is also cleared while the governor is in a join, stop or blend, so a window cannot span the governor's own transient.

### Anti-windup (the proposed correction was tested and partly rejected)

The proposal in task2.md §3 was "feed-forward priority in the clamp, back-calculate only against the feedback headroom". Tested on the 30° / 0.1 s step with the governor off (Simulated, seed 1):

| Variant | Settle within 1° | max \|integ\| |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-2B: total clamp, back-calculation on the total | 1.11 s | 0.448 N·m (at its bound) |
| FF-priority clamp, back-calculation on the total | 0.61 s | 0.448 N·m |
| FF-priority clamp, back-calculation on feedback headroom (the proposal) | **1.37 s** | 0.276 N·m |
| **FF-priority clamp, conditional integration (adopted)** | **0.60 s** | **0.037 N·m** |

- The feed-forward-priority clamp is what fixes the windup: feedback keeps its incremental authority when feed-forward alone exceeds the limit.
- Back-calculating on the headroom still drives the integrator to cancel the proportional term during long saturation.
- The remaining 0.6 s is not windup. It is a Coulomb-friction band of about 2° (0.04 N·m against the loop gain), removed by the integrator over its roughly 0.36 s time constant. The test threshold was set from that diagnosis; the first-draft 0.5 s assumed windup was the only slow mechanism.
- Governed E improves from 4.49 / 10.96° to 4.25 / 9.54° (seed 1).

### Non-finite values

- The host has a last-line guard: a non-finite torque or reference latches a rejection with its cause, before any clamp.
- The drive refuses a non-finite target. It applies 0 A in fallback mode and records a `nonfinite_target` event.
- Unchanged from before: host feedback validation, the supervisor's finite-command check, and the `invalid_command` cause, which is now tested as distinct from `cmd_timeout`.

## 6. Remaining limitations

- **Delivered motion after a tracking fault is zero until a replan.** The simulator has no planner that replans, so every run that trips ends suspended: C +35 mm with outages (parked about 44° from its 0° hold request), the faulted Monte Carlo trials, and M2.
- **Path speed now reads 0 whenever the clock is frozen** (after review, I5). The first version reported `gov_s` = 1.0 while suspended. `summarize()["speed"]` on the suspended D/E Monte Carlo trials read 0.98–0.99, which would have scored a stationary run as full speed. The §5.3 figures are unaffected (they do not use speed).
  - Whether a replan at reduced speed would succeed is not evaluated.
  - The unmodelled payload that causes these faults is the Task 3 question.
- **The catch uses the nominal model** plus a coupling bound, judged against actuator capacity. A catch under a large unmodelled load can fail and trip again. Two trips in one run occurred in 1/20 D and 3/20 E trials; three within 30 s lock the drive out, which never happened here. The lockout thresholds (3 in 30 s) are project assumptions.
- **Passive fallback is not a hold.** Under a lateral payload, the axis moves toward its passive equilibrium, about −63° for +35 mm, until caught. It travelled up to 36° in these cases and at 6.8 rad/s at most.
- **Estimate-mode cost** (5.2): C governed RMS 0.40° against 0.25°, and one yaw reduction to 0.88× during ramp-up. C sits about 0.02 N·m inside its hold budget, so estimation error of that size changes decisions.
  - The estimator's uncertainty is not propagated into the governor budget; its residual is covered only by the 0.05 N·m disturbance margin.
  - Out-of-order and burst feedback is handled by timestamp (older samples are ignored). The encoder wrap is handled in the innovation. Neither is separately stress-tested beyond the Monte Carlo bursts.
- **Plan mode** remains available and is better under a faithful yaw axis. Its assumption, that yaw follows its plan, is not verified here.
- **Incompatibility is sticky and suspends the roll request.** A borderline case, over budget but within capacity, escalates after 0.3 s, and after a passive excursion the static-hold capacity test can trigger. The 2A m3 test scenario had to move to 38 rad/s with a 40 ms blackout for this reason (§7).
- **`saturated` now means that either share of the command is clipped.** With feed-forward priority, a command can sit below the limit while feed-forward is clipped. The governor's saturation back-off and the monitor see that as saturation.
- **Carried from 2A unchanged:** zero-order-hold setpoint streams at 50 Hz or more are rejected as inconsistent plans, and fast limit toggling makes the reference chatter between boundaries. Both are declared in 2A §5.

## 8. Gate result

**Independent review round 1: PASS WITH ISSUES.** No Critical findings, 5 Important and 9 Minor; resolution in §9. Round-1 fixes were re-verified with the reviewer's own reproducer grids (§9) and a full matched re-run of the evidence (`report/packets/2B_evidence/`, regenerated after the fixes).

**Executor's gate: PASS for Packet 2B, with the limitations in §6 declared.** Against the acceptance criteria:

- **Recovery cannot bypass the tripping condition.** A tracking fault needs a host acknowledgement tied to that fault id. The ack is present only while a catch is predicted feasible, withdrawn otherwise, and required on every command of the drive's dwell. Resuming the request needs `replan()`. Repeats are bounded by the lockout.
- **Communication loss cannot be concealed.** Re-tested in all three directions: stale feedback makes the host send invalid commands, so the drive falls back. The one declared window is feedback up to 15 ms old, which is still used (review M6).
- **No unavailable future information in the claimed mode.** Estimate mode is the default. Plan mode is labelled optional and tested with plan error.
- **Fallback characterized as motion reduction.** §5.1 and §6 report its displacement and speed, and now also the hold position and the original-request error after a suspension.

A second full review round was not run. The round-1 fixes are covered by the reviewer's reproducers, now regression tests, and a delta check is recommended as part of the Packet 2C review.
