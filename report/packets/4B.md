## 4. Stress set (Simulated; §4 of the numbers file; seeds 1–3; baseline and plan)

- **Yaw plan mismatch.**
  - The baseline measures yaw and is barely affected: C at 25 ms late ×0.85 is tracked 3/3 with path RMS 0.38°.
  - The plan mode is fed the wrong yaw, and its path RMS is three times higher on the same case (1.26° against 0.38°). Its peak error rises to 3.4°.
  - With yaw 10 ms late ×1.10, the plan mode tracks and the baseline reshapes to 91 %.
  - Conclusion: neither information mode dominates. The plan mode's advantage requires the yaw to follow its plan.
- **Asymmetric loss.** This is emulated: each case drops exactly the stated share of messages in one direction, using ±0.25 ms blackout windows, because `sim.config` has no per-direction drop probability.
  - *Feedback-only* 10 % and 30 %: essentially harmless, tracked 3/3 in both modes.
  - *Command-only* 10 %: net progress 87–95 % and fallback about 2 %.
  - *Command-only* 30 %: net progress 40–48 %, 19 % of the time in drive fallback, and fallback excursions up to 9°.
  - The asymmetry follows from the design: the drive's 10 ms command timeout drops to fallback, while the host tolerates feedback up to 15 ms stale.
- **Mid-run derate to 2.4 A.** On D, the request is reshaped further (63 % progress), with no events. On A (finite) it still completes 3/3.
- **18 V bus, R +25 %.** B, D and M1 are unchanged from nominal (no voltage-limited samples).
- **Loaded recovery**, D with 0.7 kg:
  - *CoM +35 mm:* 100 ms outages give cmd_timeout → re-arm, with no tracking fault. The axis moves up to **42°** during the roughly 150 ms of fallback (§8 item 1).
  - *CoM −35 mm:* each seed trips once after re-arm and is then suspended; net progress 29–30 %.
- **Quantization near rest, 12-bit encoder.** M1 completes 3/3; the 45° hold has path RMS 0.12°.
- **Combined corners.**
  - A with 4 ms CAN, J −30 % and Kt +15 % completes 3/3, with path RMS 1.54°.
  - C at the same corner reshapes to 94 % in the baseline and is tracked in the plan mode.
  - D with 4 ms CAN, J +30 % and Kt −15 % trips once and is suspended in 3/3 seeds, with net progress 22–30 %.

## 5. Faulted D/E Monte Carlo trials, classified (Simulated; torque terms Calculated)

11 of the 40 trials fault: D 5/20 and E 6/20, as in Task 2. None was excluded.

**Rule (project assumption):** the cause is the largest term the host does not model, averaged over the 100 ms before the first trip. The candidate terms are payload, gravity-model error, coupling prediction error, inertia error and Kt error. A trial is also *limit-bound* if the command was at the current limit for at least 50 % of that window.

**Result:**
- **All 11 are unmodelled payload torque.** It is 0.19–0.34 N·m, against the next-largest term of at most 0.083 N·m (an inertia error, in D09).
- 5 of them (E00, E11, E14, E16, E17) are also limit-bound, at 57–100 % of the window at the limit.
- Coupling prediction error is zero in every trial, because D/E hold yaw still.
- 9 of 11 trip within 0.4 s of the start. D09 trips at 5.3 s and E16 at 1.57 s.

**Outcome:** every faulted trial is caught and held with the request suspended. There is no lockout in this set. Suspended time is 72–95 % of the run, or 11 % for D09, which tripped late.

**Interpretation (Calculated from the logged terms, not a hardware finding):** the D/E faults in this set come from the unknown payload, which is Task 3's question. They do not come from the coupling model or the controller structure.

## 6. Explicit infeasible cases (Simulated; §6 of the numbers file; seeds 1–3)

Neither case can be tracked, and neither is scored as tracked (0/9 for the baseline). What they show is the disposition and the motion that actually follows.

- **INF-P (physically infeasible; the load is unknown to the host):** 1.2 kg at +35 mm, derated to 2.4 A. The static load at 0°, 0.41 N·m, exceeds the actuator capacity of 0.336 N·m.
  - **Baseline:**
    1. The axis cannot hold even 0°. The governed reference steps down as the roll falls.
    2. The first tracking trip comes at about 0.16 s.
    3. After a second trip, the request is suspended ("holding at the re-arm position until replan()").
    4. The axis is held at −47° to −51°, after a peak excursion to −57° to −59° and peak speed 3.5–3.8 rad/s. It spends 96 % of the time suspended and 2 % in fallback.
  - **Legacy:** it keeps pushing at saturation after a watchdog log event and ends at −21° to −24°. That is nearer the request, but the legacy loop never discloses anything beyond the trip.
- **INF-R (infeasible by request policy; the host knows the coupling):** a roll hold under yaw ±75° at 2.8 Hz, at 2.4 A.
  - **Without yaw coordination:** the host predicts that coupling exceeds capacity and suspends the request with a coordinated stop requested, for 92 % of the run, with no drive fault. Roll stays within −2.2° to +2.7°. The simulated yaw keeps moving, because nothing in the simulation acts on the stop request.
  - **With coordination:** yaw is shrunk to a scale of 0.36–0.37 and roll stays within ±1.1°. Net progress is 78–82 %, so the run is not tracked. The delivered yaw is about 36 % of the requested amplitude.

## 7. Frequency response and generalization (§7–§8 of the numbers file)

- **Calculated vs Simulated.**
  - With Coulomb friction removed (the *bench* variant), the simulated gain and phase match the linear calculation within 0.04 in gain and 1.7° in phase, from 0.5 to 6 Hz.
  - The calculation includes the 4.0 ms governor path-clock lag and the measured torque and feedback delays. Without that lag the phase disagreed by a nearly constant 4 ms.
  - With friction, the real baseline adds 2–3° of phase lag below 2 Hz.
- **Finding (Calculated and Simulated agree):** the baseline over-tracks sines around 4–6 Hz, by 1.14–1.30× in gain (+1.2 to +2.3 dB). Requests with content at those frequencies will be amplified. The Task 2 requests are at 1–2.2 Hz, where the gain is 0.99–1.04.
- **Legacy:** 0.32× gain and −84° at 6 Hz.
- **Payload sweep (D, and M2 at 2.4 A):**
  - M2 completes up to 0.5 kg (tracked up to 0.3 kg) and fails from 0.7 kg, each time with one or two trips and then suspension.
  - D keeps 83–84 % net progress up to 0.7 kg, then collapses to 3 % at 0.9 kg and 1 % at 1.2 kg, suspended.
  - The cliff lies between 0.5 and 0.9 kg; this is the unmodelled-payload envelope.
- **Coupling scale (C, true coupling / host model):**
  - The baseline keeps 92–97 % progress up to 2×. The plan mode falls to 24 % at 2×.
  - Both are tracked only near the model (1×, and the baseline also at 1.5×).
  - At 2.5×, both lock out after repeated trips. This is outside the modelled envelope and consistent with the lockout declared in `task2.md` §8.4.

## 8. Remaining limitations and decision items for the coordinator

**Decision items.** No controller change was made; each would be a post-freeze change.
1. **Loaded fallback does not brake a moving axis.**
   - On D at +35 mm, a 100 ms command-only or both-direction outage produced up to 42° of motion during about 150 ms of drive fallback. This is roughly the sweep speed times the outage, because the passive damping of 0.05 N·m·s/rad barely decelerates the loaded axis.
   - Per plan §2, fallback is motion reduction, not a hold, so this is not a contract violation. It is a large number for hardware qualification.
   - Decision: accept and declare, or schedule a drive-side braking fallback for a later packet.
2. **Suspend-and-hold vs keep-pushing after a physical overload (INF-P).**
   - The baseline holds a position about 79° from the request. Legacy saturates and ends about 50° from it.
   - The baseline's behaviour matches its stated contract: the trip is disclosed and the request suspended until replan. Legacy's does not.
   - Confirm that this is the intended trade-off.
3. **Yaw information mode.**
   - The estimate-mode baseline pays 3–9 % of net progress on C-family cases through the yaw shrink, and loses the 4A tracked verdict in the C corner and in C with yaw 10 ms late ×1.10.
   - The plan mode is worse when the yaw deviates from its plan (the 25 ms ×0.85 case) and at 2× coupling.
   - Recommendation: keep the estimate as the claimed baseline; the plan mode stays optional.
4. **Over-tracking at 4–6 Hz** (§7). Declare it as an envelope limit, or retune in a later design comparison (Packet 4C). Not changed here.
5. **Proposed interface change (sim owner):** a per-direction drop probability in `TimingConfig`, so asymmetric loss can be random rather than emulated with windows.

**Limitations of this evidence:**
- **Sampled, not proven.** Monte Carlo and the stress cases are sampled evidence over chosen, uncalibrated ranges (seeds 1–3 or 1–5).
- **Legacy is a reconstruction** fitted in Phase 1, not the hardware controller.
- **The fault-cause rule is post hoc.** It uses the true plant state and parameters, so it is a diagnostic, never something the controller could know. A trial with two comparable terms would get only one label; none of the 11 was close (payload was at least 2.3× the next term).
- **Two diagnostic comparators are not designs:** the bench variant (friction removed in plant and host), and `use_governor=False` in the saturation step re-runs.
- **Unmet performance goals, with their delivered-motion trade-off:**
  - D and E are not tracked; they deliver 83 % and 51 % of the path time, with governed RMS 4.1–4.25°.
  - M2 is not completed at 0.7 kg and 2.4 A.
  - Command-only loss of 30 % leaves 40–48 % of the path delivered.
  - All of these are declared; none is scored as success.
- **Figure scope:** figures show seed 1 (or seed 1 of the timeline case). The tables carry the spread.

### Coordinator decisions on the items above (planner, 2026-09-24)

1. **Loaded fallback does not brake a moving axis:** up to 42° during about 150 ms of fallback on D +35 mm.
   - *Accepted and declared.* The plan defines fallback as motion reduction, not a safe pose.
   - A drive-side braking fallback is **Proposed** for the hardware qualification plan. It would change the drive supervisor and is not added to the frozen baseline.
2. **Physically infeasible load: suspend and hold, rather than keep saturating.** *Confirmed.* It is the stated contract: an infeasible request is restricted or suspended with its reason, and saturated pushing is never counted as tracking. The larger distance from the request (about 79° against legacy's 50°) is reported, not hidden.
3. **Yaw information mode.** *The causal estimate remains the claimed baseline.* The plan look-ahead remains an optional, labelled mode whose assumption (yaw follows its plan) must be verified on hardware.
4. **4–6 Hz over-tracking** (closed-loop gain 1.14–1.30). *Declared* as a property of the frozen loop. Governed references rarely carry that band, and the plan keeps the 4.46 Hz design unless evidence justifies re-derivation. Packet 4C may revisit it.

The proposed per-direction drop probability in `TimingConfig` is noted for the simulator owner. Blackout-share emulation is accepted for 4B and declared.
