## 2. Saturation

### 2a. Calculated

- A saturating clamp behaves like a loop-gain reduction (describing-function gain between 0 and 1).
- **Nominal gravity stiffness:** the linear loop is stable for every gain reduction (no lower limit). Saturation alone cannot destabilise it; it can only slow recovery.
- **1 kg lateral payload at ±80° (k_g = -0.32 N·m/rad):** the plant is locally unstable and the loop needs at least **0.39×** of its designed gain. Sustained deep saturation there *can* lose the position. That is why the governor budgets torque to stay out of saturation, and why the drive watchdog and fallback exist.
- Mechanisms: the governor budgets torque at 80 % of the reported limit minus the 0.05 N·m disturbance bound; feed-forward has priority in the clamp and the integrator uses conditional integration (no integration deeper into a clip, Packet 2B); the integral torque is bounded to ±Kt·I_limit; the drive re-clamps the delayed target to the newest limit.

### 2b. Simulated: 30° requested in 0.1 s (needs ≈ 1.2 N·m, capacity 0.448 N·m)

Nominal plant, 14-bit encoder, random CAN, 3.2 A limit. Rows B–D switch the governor off *only* to force saturation. B and C use a drive without the tracking watchdog, to isolate the integrator.

## 4. Quantization

### 4a. Calculated

## 2. Saturation

### 2a. Calculated

- A saturating clamp behaves like a loop-gain reduction (describing-function gain between 0 and 1).
- **Nominal gravity stiffness:** the linear loop is stable for every gain reduction (no lower limit). Saturation alone cannot destabilise it; it can only slow recovery.
- **1 kg lateral payload at ±80° (k_g = -0.32 N·m/rad):** the plant is locally unstable and the loop needs at least **0.39×** of its designed gain. Sustained deep saturation there *can* lose the position. That is why the governor budgets torque to stay out of saturation, and why the drive watchdog and fallback exist.
- Mechanisms: the governor budgets torque at 80 % of the reported limit minus the 0.05 N·m disturbance bound; feed-forward has priority in the clamp and the integrator uses conditional integration (no integration deeper into a clip, Packet 2B); the integral torque is bounded to ±Kt·I_limit; the drive re-clamps the delayed target to the newest limit.

### 2b. Simulated: 30° requested in 0.1 s (needs ≈ 1.2 N·m, capacity 0.448 N·m)

Nominal plant, 14-bit encoder, random CAN, 3.2 A limit. Rows B–D switch the governor off *only* to force saturation. B and C use a drive without the tracking watchdog, to isolate the integrator.

## 4. Quantization

- The yaw coupling feed-forward differentiates the yaw encoder twice through the Kalman estimate, so yaw quantization reaches the command as current jitter (4b).

## 2. Saturation

### 2a. Calculated

- A saturating clamp behaves like a loop-gain reduction (describing-function gain between 0 and 1).
- **Nominal gravity stiffness:** the linear loop is stable for every gain reduction (no lower limit). Saturation alone cannot destabilise it; it can only slow recovery.
- **1 kg lateral payload at ±80° (k_g = -0.32 N·m/rad):** the plant is locally unstable and the loop needs at least **0.39×** of its designed gain. Sustained deep saturation there *can* lose the position. That is why the governor budgets torque to stay out of saturation, and why the drive watchdog and fallback exist.
- Mechanisms: the governor budgets torque at 80 % of the reported limit minus the 0.05 N·m disturbance bound; feed-forward has priority in the clamp and the integrator uses conditional integration (no integration deeper into a clip, Packet 2B); the integral torque is bounded to ±Kt·I_limit; the drive re-clamps the delayed target to the newest limit.

## 4. Quantization

## 2. Saturation

### 2a. Calculated

- A saturating clamp behaves like a loop-gain reduction (describing-function gain between 0 and 1).
- **Nominal gravity stiffness:** the linear loop is stable for every gain reduction (no lower limit). Saturation alone cannot destabilise it; it can only slow recovery.
- **1 kg lateral payload at ±80° (k_g = -0.32 N·m/rad):** the plant is locally unstable and the loop needs at least **0.39×** of its designed gain. Sustained deep saturation there *can* lose the position. That is why the governor budgets torque to stay out of saturation, and why the drive watchdog and fallback exist.
- Mechanisms: the governor budgets torque at 80 % of the reported limit minus the 0.05 N·m disturbance bound; feed-forward has priority in the clamp and the integrator uses conditional integration (no integration deeper into a clip, Packet 2B); the integral torque is bounded to ±Kt·I_limit; the drive re-clamps the delayed target to the newest limit.

# Task 2 — robustness evidence

Generated by `python -m exp.task2_robustness`. **Calculated** = roots of the linearised loop with a Padé-6 delay; **Simulated** = nonlinear multi-rate simulator with the unmodified baseline unless a row says otherwise. Neither is a hardware observation.

Controller under test: K = 0.7553 N·m/rad, ωc = 28.0 rad/s, α = 16, integral zero 2.8 rad/s, 500 Hz.

**Provenance:** baseline fingerprint `7d857df507c389c9` (exp/evidence.py BASELINE_SOURCES); git `unavailable (not a git checkout)`, baseline sources not checked against git (the fingerprint is the authority). Frozen baseline (Packet 2C); the same code hash is published in [task2_numbers.md](task2_numbers.md).

**Information mode:** the default causal mode. Yaw coupling feed-forward is predicted 4 ms ahead from the yaw encoder in the delayed feedback (Kalman estimate, Packet 2B); the yaw plan is not read. Rows in §1 and §3 that hold roll with yaw still are unaffected by the mode.

## 1. Delay

### 1a. Calculated: closed-loop roots of the linearised loop

Pure delay T is the total of CAN, compute, sample age, zero-order hold and the 1 ms command delay; the 1.2 ms current lag is modelled separately. The simulator's measured mean is about 6 ms, the normal worst case about 7 ms, and 11 ms if every message is in a 4 ms burst.

| pure delay ms | slowest root Re (1/s) | min damping ratio ζ | gain may rise by (dB) | gain may fall by (dB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | -2.3 | 0.49 | 17.2 | no lower limit |
| 6 | -2.3 | 0.48 | 14.5 | no lower limit |
| 7 | -2.3 | 0.48 | 13.5 | no lower limit |
| 9 | -2.3 | 0.47 | 11.7 | no lower limit |
| 11 | -2.3 | 0.46 | 10.2 | no lower limit |
| 14 | -2.3 | 0.45 | 8.4 | no lower limit |

**Delay margin (calculated): 37.8 ms** of pure delay before the linear loop loses stability, versus 7 ms normal worst case and 11 ms all-burst. The root locations reproduce the frequency-domain margins in `ctrl/loopshape.py` to 0.01 dB. With the nominal gravity stiffness the loop has no lower gain limit: any gain reduction (for example a saturating clamp) leaves it stable. That changes when a payload makes the stiffness negative; see §2 and §3.

### 1b. Simulated cross-check of the calculation

The same controller runs in the multi-rate simulator (500 Hz host, 1 kHz drive, CAN in both directions, 1 ms command delay, current lag). Friction, voltage limit, d(t) and quantization are removed, and current limits are lifted, so the result isolates delay. Its feedback gain is scaled until a 2° offset stops decaying (bisection, 9 steps). The analytic prediction uses the pipeline delay measured in that run.

| CAN latency | measured pipeline delay ms | analytic critical gain × | simulated critical gain × | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.2 ms each way | 6.00 | 5.34 | 5.31 | -0 % |
| 4.0 ms each way | 11.00 | 3.24 | 3.23 | -0 % |

Agreement within the bisection resolution and the ZOH approximation means the linear model's delay accounting is trustworthy enough to use for the parameter grid below. (A similar test on the legacy PD loop is part of `tests/test_sim.py`.)

## 2. Saturation

### 2a. Calculated

- A saturating clamp behaves like a loop-gain reduction (describing-function gain between 0 and 1).
- **Nominal gravity stiffness:** the linear loop is stable for every gain reduction (no lower limit). Saturation alone cannot destabilise it; it can only slow recovery.
- **1 kg lateral payload at ±80° (k_g = -0.32 N·m/rad):** the plant is locally unstable and the loop needs at least **0.39×** of its designed gain. Sustained deep saturation there *can* lose the position. That is why the governor budgets torque to stay out of saturation, and why the drive watchdog and fallback exist.
- Mechanisms: the governor budgets torque at 80 % of the reported limit minus the 0.05 N·m disturbance bound; feed-forward has priority in the clamp and the integrator uses conditional integration (no integration deeper into a clip, Packet 2B); the integral torque is bounded to ±Kt·I_limit; the drive re-clamps the delayed target to the newest limit.

### 2b. Simulated: 30° requested in 0.1 s (needs ≈ 1.2 N·m, capacity 0.448 N·m)

Nominal plant, 14-bit encoder, random CAN, 3.2 A limit. Rows B–D switch the governor off *only* to force saturation. B and C use a drive without the tracking watchdog, to isolate the integrator.

| configuration | first within 1° of 30° (s) | overshoot ° | last time outside ±1° (s) | % time at limit | max |integrator| N·m | supervisor events |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. governor ON, real supervisor (the baseline) | 0.17 | 1.1 | 0.22 | 0.0 | 0.017 | 0 |
| B. governor OFF, anti-windup ON, no watchdog | 0.60 | 0.6 | 0.60 | 2.0 | 0.037 | 0 |
| C. governor OFF, anti-windup OFF, no watchdog | 0.18 | 1.5 | 1.61 | 1.8 | 0.100 | 0 |
| D. governor OFF, anti-windup ON, real supervisor | 0.60 | 0.6 | 0.60 | 2.0 | 0.037 | 0 |

Reading (checked against the traces in the figure):
- **A (normal baseline):** the governor reshapes the request; the loop reaches the target with no time at the current limit and no supervisor events.
- **B vs C (anti-windup ablation, governor off):** with anti-windup the integrator stays near zero through the inertial feed-forward clip (max |integrator| column), so the approach is not slowed by unwinding. Pre-2B, back-calculation on the total clamp drove it to its −Kt·I_limit bound and settling took 1.11 s (Packet 2B §3). The remaining approach time is the ~2° Coulomb-friction band removed by the integrator.
- **D (real supervisor, governor off):** no supervisor event: the tracking error stays inside the 12°/40 ms watchdog. Pre-2B the same case tripped and re-armed repeatedly; the feed-forward-priority clamp keeps feedback authority through the clip. Had it tripped, the request would be suspended until a replan (Packet 2B).
- These ungoverned cases are deliberately off-nominal: they force saturation to exercise the anti-windup and the fault path, which the governed baseline (row A) avoids.

## 3. Uncertain parameters

## 4. Quantization

### 4a. Calculated

- One count is 3.83e-04 rad (0.0220°).
- The feedback's high-frequency gain turns one count into about **33 mA** of current step: 1.0 % of the 3.2 A limit.
- Differentiating one count at 500 Hz gives 0.192 rad/s, ten times the 0.02 rad/s friction scale. That is why no raw velocity estimate is fed to friction compensation or to the feedback law. (A filtered roll-velocity estimate exists since Packet 2B, used only to plan a post-fault catch.)
- The yaw coupling feed-forward differentiates the yaw encoder twice through the Kalman estimate, so yaw quantization reaches the command as current jitter (4b).

### 4b. Simulated: same runs at three encoder resolutions

24-bit is effectively unquantized. Run B includes d(t); the 45° hold has d(t) switched off so that only quantization, friction and CAN timing act. Current jitter is the standard deviation of the 1 ms current increment (a high-pass measure). Hunting is the peak-to-peak encoder reading over the last 3 s of the hold, in degrees; it is similar at every resolution, so it comes from friction and integral action (stick-slip), not from the encoder.

Run B current jitter with the baseline's yaw estimate: 7.6 / 31.0 / 117.3 mA at 24/14/12 bits; with the plan look-ahead instead: 7.1 / 7.7 / 9.4 mA. The growth with coarser encoders is therefore mostly the yaw estimate differentiating a quantized yaw signal. At 14 bits it is 1.0 % of the 3.2 A limit per 1 ms step (standard deviation).
